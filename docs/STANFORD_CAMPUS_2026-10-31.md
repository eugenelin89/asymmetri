# Stanford Virtual Campus — implementation and evidence

Independent campus at `/labs/stanford`, built on the UBC/CWRU interaction model. The [initial release is live and verified](STANFORD_RELEASE_2026-10-31.md). This document records the implemented scope without claiming photorealism, measured interiors or real-time whereabouts.

## Campus and architectural references

The versioned OpenStreetMap snapshot supplies 4,581 building parts, 29,635 surfaces, 7,144 trees, 3,291 pedestrian routes, 156 entrances and 3,398 road parts. Stanford's 32 enterable landmarks have individual procedural architectural models, furnished public cores, collision geometry, floors and floor guides. Twelve outdoor venue groups retain source placement, including a separate Redwood City rowing/sailing waterfront approximately 9.6 km from the campus origin.

Memorial Church has its triple portals, mosaic interpretation, nave, pews, stained-glass interpretation, timber trusses and organ. Main Quad has sandstone arcades around mapped courtyard space. Hoover Tower includes an 18-flight walk to its observation deck. Green Library and Bing Wing provide reading spaces and multiple floors. Art, music, engineering, student life, residence and indoor athletics buildings have distinct arrangements. Branner's working bedroom/bathroom doors and two-stop lift serve explicitly invented demonstration rooms.

`projects/stanford/architecture-sources.json` separates observed architectural features, photo references and interpreted layouts. Official Stanford descriptions, accessible-campus maps, architect pages, Library of Congress imagery and venue photographs informed original geometry. Photos are reference-only and are not redistributed. Terrain is flat; many heights, room layouts and furnishings are inferred. Many mapped background buildings remain exterior-only. This is a substantial stylized reconstruction, not a surveyed digital twin.

## Controls, profiles and editions

UBC parity includes first/third person, aerial orbit, geographic map, destination search, entrance visits, walking guidance, contextual encounters, bounded conversations, personal appearance, public visitor profiles, photo mode, full view, keyboard/touch controls, quality settings and reduced motion. Closing panels returns focus to the walking canvas; map dialogs restore their opener. Search/profile text input remains independent of movement keys.

The Stanford community namespace has independent routes, ownership cookie and private file storage. Visitors can create, edit, recover, report and delete their own public character. Other visitors see those submitted profiles on refresh. Backend tests cover ownership, immediate publication, recovery, moderation, removal, validation, origin checks, rate limiting and private cookie/storage boundaries. Production operation requires persistent Node storage through `STANFORD_PROFILE_DIR` and the same maintenance discipline as UBC. A disposable local HTTP fixture additionally passed publish/list/edit, unauthorized edit rejection, cross-origin rejection, recovery, reporting and deletion; it was removed afterward. No production data or private credentials were used for validation.

Halloween automatically runs October 24 through November 7 inclusive in `America/Los_Angeles`. Base/Halloween can be selected manually; the automatic option restores calendar behavior. In-season saved choices expire November 8. December 1–31 is reserved for Christmas, but Christmas is unavailable and currently falls back to base.

Halloween includes 22 monster types, 24 original comic/manga/robot costume visitors, pumpkins, indoor decorations, moonlight and bats. The optional Friday-the-13th-style hockey-mask pursuer carries a prop knife, follows clear outdoor routes, yields to peers, stops short of the player and waits outside buildings. There is no damage or attack mechanic.

Original Web Audio synthesis supplies minor-key drones, wind, bells, wolf howls, and nearby ghost cries/screams with stereo positioning and distance attenuation. Enter/Sound gestures unlock playback. Mute/volume are available, base mode fades the soundtrack, and hidden tabs suspend audio. Browser telemetry confirmed a running context and scheduled atmospheric cues; subjective speaker audition was not performed. No external recording or voice imitation is bundled.

## Performance and validation

Collision-checked seasonal layouts, all 1,402 people routes and a 13,788-node/29,420-directed-edge pedestrian graph are compiled ahead of runtime. Their provenance hashes bind them to source geography and collision-model inputs. Runtime scene construction yields between batches and reports progress. Distant building proxies, tree tiles, crowd streaming and adaptive resolution bound rendering work. Auto quality additionally disables shadows and reduces distant detail after four consecutive sub-24-FPS measurement windows; changing quality resets that fallback.

All 140 Stanford tests passed on Node 24.10.0, including all 32 entrances against the complete campus collision world, architecture and stairs, sports seating, Branner doors/lift, roster routes, seasonal dates, pursuit clearance, sound proximity and community security boundaries. TypeScript/ESLint passed with the pre-existing tutorial navigation warning. The Vinext and standard Next webpack production builds passed against the committed CWRU/navigation baseline. The integrated Next prebuild passed all 459 tests: 69 Surrey, 27 Richmond, 134 UBC, 89 CWRU and 140 Stanford. Local serving of the final production bundle additionally passed eight page checks, 20 static-asset requests, three exact-hash Stanford geographic assets, campus GIS/sitemap entries, protected footer links, the tutorial canonical, and the public profile feed with no-store headers. Additional final integration checks are recorded in the validation gallery and engineering journal.

Browser validation used the local Next webpack preview at desktop 1280×720, tablet 768×1024 and mobile 390×844. No horizontal document overflow or broken images appeared at the measured widths. First- and third-person WASD resumed after closing Explore without an extra scene click; Escape returned focus to the canvas and search typing remained functional. The Memorial Church entrance was walked through. Base/automatic Halloween switching, sound settings, map and profile form loaded successfully. See `docs/validation/stanford/` for unretouched captures.

Frame rate on the shared test host was variable and low under contention. After the Auto fallback reduced distant detail and shadows, a Halloween observation reached 56 FPS with 224 draw calls and 1,085,051 triangles; this is not a performance certification. Users can lower quality or population. A later observation with the expanded roster and concurrent host work was 12 FPS; results remain hardware/load-dependent. Physical-device audio, mobile GPU coverage, and exhaustive manual traversal of every furnished room remain unverified.

No dependencies were added. Stanford source, geographic assets and validation evidence are separate from concurrent UBC/CWRU changes. The separately authorized initial production activation and live acceptance are recorded in the release receipt. Shared public review and private analytics components are being developed by the UBC owner. After their source is committed, this Stanford workstream owns its own component integration, testing and follow-up deployment; they are excluded from the initial campus artifact.

## Shared analytics integration handoff

The shared private analytics module is owned by the UBC workstream and must be imported only after its source is committed. Stanford currently has no analytics integration; its own workstream will add and validate it in a separate follow-up release. `Campus.tsx` owns `started`, `ready`, `error`, `panel`, `photo` and `mode`. The loaded-session signal is `started && ready && !error`; the stricter active-scene signal is `started && ready && !error && panel === null && !photo && mode !== "map"`. The engine pauses movement while a panel is open, and walking input applies only to first/third-person modes. The shared tracker should separately enforce document visibility, activity/idle limits, user opt-out and server-clock duration caps. There is no need to access private engine state or character/profile identifiers.
